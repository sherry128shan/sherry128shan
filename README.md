## My product lens

I am drawn to the moment a product stops being a feature list and starts feeling like it understands someone. I work from real behavior, not assumptions: find the friction, identify the decision that matters, make the path lighter, then let usage tell us what to improve.

For me, AI is not the point of the experience. It is the material: useful only when it helps a user move from *“I’m stuck”* to *“I know what to do next.”*

## How I build

| I look for | I try to create |
| :-- | :-- |
| A user hesitation | A next step that feels natural |
| A messy, high-effort workflow | A clear loop with sensible defaults and recovery paths |
| A clever AI capability | A reliable interaction with the right context and guardrails |
| A metric that moves | A behavioral explanation for why it moved |

`OBSERVE` → `FRAME THE REAL PROBLEM` → `MAKE THE SIMPLEST BET` → `SHIP` → `LEARN`

## What this looks like in practice

- I turn fuzzy AI outputs into product experiences with clear modes, context, and feedback loops.
- I care about the last 10% of a journey: the wording, the fallback, the timing, and the feeling of control that determines whether a feature is actually used.
- I can move from research and product framing to a clickable prototype or lightweight build quickly - then use evidence rather than enthusiasm to decide what earns a second iteration.
- I like products that are quiet but consequential: they reduce mental load, respect attention, and make progress visible.

## A few signals I value

Adoption over novelty · clarity over feature volume · trust over one-off delight · learning velocity over perfect first drafts.
